Piano Model X — Sampled

A Yamaha C5, recorded. One dedicated sample per key per velocity layer — 88 × 16, repitched off the Salamander library with the instrument's own body response held still — played by an engine with per-key velocity response, stereo spread, a geometric room, and sympathetic resonance that accumulates while the pedal is down.

The library streams as you play: the first notes are ready in a few seconds, the rest arrives behind you. Connect a MIDI keyboard before or after starting, or play with z…m / q…u. space is the sustain pedal.

Keyboard — click to play (hit high on the key for more velocity), shift-click to lift its dampers silently

Hear the sympathetic resonance: shift-click C4, E4 and G4 to raise their dampers without striking them — they turn blue — then play C2 hard. The chord answers on its own, and it answers louder the harder you hit. Now hold the sustain pedal and play the same note repeatedly: the resonance piles up, because each strike adds to an energy accumulator that only leaks away at each string's own measured decay rate. Lift the pedal and every damper falls at once.

Velocity response — C4

Level comes from the curve; timbre comes from which of the sixteen recordings the velocity reaches for. They are separate on purpose — changing the curve never changes the tone, and changing the layer bias never changes the level. Both are per key: draw them on the right.

Output & stereo

Every key gets its own place across the image, bass to treble, as a rotation of that key's recorded stereo picture rather than a pan — so the treble does not arrive mono and hard right. The recordings are all from one microphone position, so this is putting back something the soundboard had and the microphones could not.

Room

Not a captured impulse response — an image-source model of a shoebox for the early reflections and an eight-line feedback delay network for the tail, the same room the modelled variant uses, rendered to an impulse and convolved. Which is why the controls are geometry: an IR library cannot move you across the room.

Sympathetic resonance

Which strings answer is decided by partial coincidence, with each string's real inharmonicity and the bandwidth implied by its measured decay — so a fifth answers strongly and a tritone barely. Selectivity widens that bandwidth: at 1× it is physical and almost nothing resonates unless it is in tune to a cent, which is why every instrument that does this has to open it up. Build with velocity is the exponent on velocity: 2 is the energy a hammer delivers.

Note attack

Zero by default and doing nothing — the recording already has a hammer in it. Raise it to take the knock off the front of a note.

Damper fall

Duration is per key — the Damper fall curve on the right. This is the shape, and it is what decides whether a released chord sounds stopped or swallowed. The same shape stops a sympathetic voice, because it is the same damper.

Release sample attack

Release samples start on top of a note that is still sounding, so switching one on at full level is a click in the middle of a decaying chord.

Release sample release

Applied at the tail of the release sample, so it lands rather than stops.

Release level against how long the key was held

The damper-release sound is the sound of a damper stopping something, so it depends on how much is left to stop — and a string four seconds into its decay has very little. The dashed blue line is C4's own decay, measured at build time: trace it and the law is physically right for that key. Salamander's SFZ does this as a flat dB per second; a real decay is not a straight line, and the first second matters far more than the eighth. The key-up thud does not follow by default — a key comes up the same way however long it was down — but the last slider is there if you disagree.

Mechanics

The sustain pedal is continuous, not a switch: half-pedalling shortens a note without stopping it, and CC 64 is read across its whole range. The damper-fall sound is picked by velocity and scaled by how long the key was held — a string ringing for four seconds has less left in it to stop. The limiter is a seatbelt, not a sound: it is doing nothing until eighty-eight undamped strings would have run out of headroom.
