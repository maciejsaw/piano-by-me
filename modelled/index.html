Hear the sympathetic resonance: shift-click C4 to hold it silently (its dampers lift but no hammer strikes), then play C3 hard and release. C4 keeps ringing — driven entirely through the bridge.

Instrument

The dispersion chain that gives a string its inharmonicity is a cascade of allpass sections, and a short one cannot hold the partial ladder of a note with hundreds of partials: untreated, A0's are out by 400 cents by 2 kHz, which is the metallic edge on the bottom two octaves. Notes at or below this one get a chain long enough to hold them (up to 6 kHz, within 25 cents) when they are struck. Drag to the far left to turn it off, to the far right to give it to the whole keyboard. Costs DSP load in the register it covers, so it is only handed to strings you actually strike, never to ones ringing sympathetically.

Velocity response

Volume

Off, a note is as loud as its hammer speed makes it — the physics. On, it lands where this curve says, in dB below a full-velocity strike of the same key, and the hammer speed only decides the timbre. The default is the physics' own curve, so switching it on changes nothing until you move a point. Click to add a point, drag to move, double-click to remove; pull the far-left point to the bottom for a true pianissimo. The blue line is the last note you struck.

Hammer hardness

Added to the felt's hardness for each strike, in the same units as the Felt hardness voicing curve below (+0.1 here is +0.1 there). Up is harder felt: a shorter contact, a brighter and louder note. Flat at zero is the voicing as fitted; drawing it up toward the right makes loud playing brighter than hammer speed alone does, down toward the left softens pianissimo.

Output EQ — after everything, including the room

Shelves at the ends, two bells in the middle: body around 300 Hz where a piano gets boxy, presence around 3 kHz where the hammer lives. The curve is drawn from the filters' own frequency response, so what is on screen is what is running.

Case & body — everything downstream of the bridge

The string model stops at the bridge. This is the rest: plate radiation efficiency, the case as a baffle, the enclosed air as real box modes computed from these dimensions, and one lid reflection. Measured against a real grand, a model with no body is about 22 dB bass-heavy and treble-shy — the single largest error in the instrument. Because this sits downstream of every coupling path, making it linear costs nothing: sympathetic resonance is unaffected.

Room — stereo, early reflections and tail kept separate

Two parts, because the ear uses them for different things. The early reflections are real paths off real surfaces — the source mirrored in each wall, arrival time from the distance, level from the inverse of it, absorption from how many surfaces the path touched — and they are what tell you the size of the room and where you are standing in it. The tail is a feedback delay network, because by then the reflections are too dense to be worth counting. Left and right differ because the two ears are at different distances from every image, not because anything was widened. The room is NOT in the mono path the fitting tools use: a room in front of a measurement fits the instrument to the room.

Parameter editor — slider is a global offset, chart is per key

Centre is the fitted curve, always — every number here is a DEPARTURE from what the fitting tools measured, never a replacement for it, so there is a defined way back from any edit. Precision zooms the slider and the chart together; the stored offset is absolute, so drawing finely on one note cannot rescale what was drawn coarsely before. Offsets are in octaves of the value for rates, levels and durations — the ear hears those as ratios, and a linear knob on a decay time is not symmetric about its centre.

Coupling

Coupling is expressed as the fraction of each string's own loss that goes into the bridge instead of into internal damping — so it can never destabilise, and the decay time you ask for stays the decay time you get. Unison coupling drives beating and double decay; bridge coupling drives the sympathetic halo.
